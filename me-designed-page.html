Profile

I am a final-year BEng Electrical & Electronic Engineering student at the University of Nottingham Ningbo China. My work sits at the boundary between the analogue world and the digital one: sampling a current shunt accurately enough for field-oriented control, keeping a control loop deterministic when the scheduler is not, and reasoning about why a robot that worked on the bench drifts in the corridor.

Three things keep drawing me back. Determinism — a 1 kHz current loop has to finish its work in the time it was given, every time. Diagnosis — most embedded failures are not algorithmic but electrical, and the oscilloscope usually settles in a minute what a week of code review cannot. Restraint — the best board is often the one with fewer parts, and the best firmware is the one that fits in a page.

Alongside hardware I have worked on longitudinal vehicle modelling and car-following simulation, which is where my interest in autonomous systems became concrete: the same loop that holds a motor at 3 000 rpm also holds a vehicle at a safe headway.

Selected work

  1. 2026 Firmware · Hardware

    Field-oriented control for a BLDC drive

    Bare-metal STM32F4 firmware driving a three-phase inverter at 20 kHz PWM. Implemented Clarke/Park transforms, two current PI loops and a speed loop, with an SPI magnetic encoder for rotor angle and CAN frames for telemetry.

    Hardest part Current-sense gain drift pushed the d-axis reference off zero at high duty; fixed by re-zeroing offsets inside the PWM dead-time window instead of at start-up only.

  2. 2026 Robotics · Systems

    Differential-drive rover with ROS 2

    A two-wheel rover built around an ESP32 bridge and a Raspberry Pi running ROS 2. Wheel odometry is published from quadrature counts, fused with an IMU, and consumed by a Nav2 stack fed by a 2D LiDAR scan.

    Hardest part Odometry drifted on carpet because the two wheels carried unequal weight; corrected by calibrating an effective wheel radius per side against a straight-line ground truth.

  3. 2026 Simulation · Python

    Car-following: Gipps and kNN driver models

    Longitudinal vehicle simulation comparing an analytic Gipps car-following model against a k-nearest-neighbour driver model trained on trajectory data, evaluated with time–space diagrams and fundamental-diagram plots.

    Hardest part The kNN model reproduced plausible headways but its accelerations were discontinuous; a first-order lag on the commanded acceleration was needed before the trajectory stopped looking like a step function.

  4. 2025 Hardware · PCB

    Low-power sensor node

    Four-layer node with an I²C sensor bus, a buck converter and a microcontroller sleep schedule, designed in KiCad and brought up on a reflowed prototype.

    Hardest part The first revision drew 400 µA in sleep — an unconfigured GPIO holding a regulator enable pin; the second revision reduced it to 12 µA.
